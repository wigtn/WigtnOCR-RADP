\begin{abstract}
Document parsers are often selected by intrinsic measures of transcription fidelity and boundary quality, even though their outputs ultimately serve retrieval. We show that this mismatch can change deployment decisions. In a table-enabled KoGovDoc-RAG audit, our parser (Prod) achieves a Hit@1 of $0.549$, versus $0.123$ for MinerU---a $42.6$ percentage-point ($4.47\times$) gap. Yet MinerU ranks among the highest on Boundary Clarity in our candidate pool. We define the Retrieval-Conditional Parsing Score (\rcps{}), a training-free protocol that ranks parser--chunker combinations on a held-out question--answer (Q--A) retrieval probe. It uses format-normalised exact-span relevance averaged across retrievers. A retriever-free coverage diagnostic distinguishes exact spans absent from parser output from spans split at chunk boundaries. Under exact matching, $20.2\%$ of reference spans are absent from Prod's output, whereas at most $2.3\%$ are split. Within our candidate pools, the \rcps{} range across parsers evaluated on 294 pages is $0.447$, compared with $0.058$ across chunkers. On source-aligned OHR-Bench data, semantic noise reduces retrieval while Boundary Clarity remains stable or changes non-monotonically. On an audited six-domain compatibility subset, two direct preference optimization checkpoints improve Hit@5 over Prod by $0.95$ and $1.15$ percentage points. We release KoGovDoc-RAG evaluation files and an \rcps{} reference implementation.
\end{abstract}

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems for documents often begin with scanned or born-digital PDFs. A parser converts each page to text before chunking, embedding, and retrieval, so omitted or corrupted content can limit downstream retrieval. Parsers therefore ought to be evaluated by the retrieval they enable. In practice, however, teams commonly choose among them using intrinsic measures such as edit distance to a reference transcription or Boundary Clarity (BC) \citep{zhao2025moc}. These measures capture reference fidelity or boundary structure, but neither directly measures retrievability.

We encountered this mismatch while building a production RAG system for Korean government documents. The submitted MinerU-off output \citep{mineru2025} has one of the highest BC scores in our pool ($0.72$). Separately, in a table-enabled audit, MinerU-on achieves a Hit@1 of $0.123$, whereas Prod achieves $0.549$---a $42.6$ percentage-point ($4.47\times$) gap. Limitations describes the rerun environment. On aligned OHR-Bench data \citep{zhang2024ohr}, controlled semantic corruption likewise reduces retrieval while BC remains nearly fixed or changes non-monotonically.

Two further findings bound the practical response. On Prod output, $20.2\%$ of reference spans have no normalised exact match before chunking, whereas at most $2.3\%$ are split by a chunk boundary. Parser-side preference training produces much smaller gains: two audited checkpoints improve Hit@5 by approximately one percentage point on a six-domain OHR-Bench compatibility subset.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We (i) document a mismatch between intrinsic parsing metrics and retrieval (C1); (ii) introduce a retriever-free diagnostic that distinguishes exact spans absent from parser output from spans split across chunk boundaries (C2); (iii) define \rcps{}, a training-free protocol for ranking parsers and chunkers on a held-out retrieval probe (C3); and (iv) quantify the observed gains from retrieval-aware parser training and their uncertainty (C4). Our primary scope is answer-span retrievability, not generated-answer quality. Appendix~\ref{app:e2e} provides a bounded, three-parser end-to-end check of the top selection.

The repository contains the frozen \textbf{KoGovDoc-RAG} evaluation files---663 question--answer pairs over 294 pages---and a reference implementation of \rcps{}.\footnote{\url{https://github.com/wigtn/WigtnOCR-RADP}}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Prior evidence.} OHR-Bench \citep{zhang2024ohr} measures how optical character recognition (OCR) noise propagates through RAG, while EnterpriseDocBench \citep{enterprisedocbench2026} and concurrent studies \citep{goodocr2026,song2026ocr} report weak links between parsing quality and retrieval. Most parsing benchmarks still rank systems by intrinsic fidelity \citep{ouyang2025omnidocbench}. Vision-based retrieval \citep{faysse2024colpali} avoids text parsing altogether and is therefore complementary to the text pipelines studied here. To our knowledge, prior work has not combined a reusable parser-selection protocol with a diagnostic that distinguishes exact spans absent from parser output from spans split across chunk boundaries.

\paragraph{Training.} Retrieval-oriented training has been applied to chunking \citep{jina2024late,duarte2024lumber,zhao2024meta,zhao2025moc,singh2024chunkrag}, embedding \citep{conti2025insent,zhao2025lmar}, and retriever or reader components \citep{nguyen2024rewardrag,chia2025mlongdoc,yan2025rpo}. These interventions target downstream layers: M-LongDoc \citep{chia2025mlongdoc} makes a reader robust to retrieved context, whereas InSeNT \citep{conti2025insent} trains contextual embeddings. Parser training has instead used edit-distance and layout rewards \citep{infinity_parser} or query guidance for efficient RAG \citep{agenticocr}. We intervene at the earlier parser layer by testing a retrieval reward and designing a fidelity-based control. Because aligned per-Q--A results for the control are unavailable, C4 does not report a matched quantitative comparison. Our main contributions (C1--C3) require no training.

\section{Two Tools: Selection and Diagnosis}

\subsection{\rcps{}: Retrieval-Conditional Parsing Score}
\label{sec:rcps}

\rcps{} ranks parsers by the retrieval performance of their output rather than by its appearance. It is a protocol built on standard mean reciprocal rank (MRR), not a new similarity function. The protocol has three design choices. First, it is \emph{extrinsic}: candidates are evaluated on a held-out Q--A probe over the parsed corpus. Second, it is \emph{retriever-averaged}: scores are averaged across a specified set of retrievers to reduce dependence on any single one. Here, a retriever is the shared nearest-neighbour search pipeline instantiated with a particular embedder. Third, the protocol uses \emph{format-normalised exact-span relevance}: a chunk is relevant only when it comes from the answer page and contains the answer after shared whitespace and Markdown normalisation. We use \rcps{} \emph{score} for the aggregate in Equation~\ref{eq:rcps} and \rcps{} \emph{protocol} for this complete procedure.

Given a parser $P$, a chunker $C$, a Q--A set $D = \{(q_i, a_i, \text{page}_i)\}$, a set of retrievers $R$, and cutoffs $K$, \rcps{} averages MRR over all retriever--cutoff pairs:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rcps}
\begin{aligned}
\text{RCPS}(P,C)
  &= \frac{1}{|R|\,|K|} \sum_{r \in R} \sum_{k \in K} \\
  &\quad \text{MRR}@k(r, C(P), D).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here, $C(P)$ denotes the corpus obtained by parsing with $P$ and then chunking with $C$. We instantiate $R$ with BGE-M3 \citep{chen2024bgem3}, multilingual-e5-large \citep{wang2024me5}, and Qwen3-Embedding-8B \citep{qwen3emb}, and use $K = \{1,5,10\}$. Averaging over cutoffs avoids tying selection to one retrieval depth. Figure~\ref{fig:protocol} shows the workflow.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.6\columnwidth]{fig_rcps_protocol.png}
\caption{\rcps{} wraps standard MRR with a held-out Q--A probe, retriever averaging, and format-normalised relevance. The resulting score ranks candidate parsers and chunkers without training.}
\label{fig:protocol}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Execution.} First, construct a representative held-out Q--A probe whose answer spans occur verbatim in its reference pages. Our probe is model-generated, and an LLM judge evaluated a 100-pair sample (\S\ref{sec:setup}). Parse the evaluation pages once with each candidate, then chunk, embed, and retrieve with every member of $R$. For $m$ parsers, $c$ chunkers, and $|R|$ retrievers, the protocol requires $m$ parsing runs and $mc|R|$ indexing-and-retrieval evaluations.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} Equation~\ref{eq:rcps} yields a ranking that can inform deployment. The protocol requires neither model training nor manual chunk-level relevance labels, because relevance follows from the gold span and source page. Fixing $C$ ranks parsers; fixing $P$ ranks chunkers. The score is meaningful only relative to the candidate pool and probe.

\subsection{Coverage Diagnostic: Parser or Chunker?}
\label{sec:coverage}

A low \rcps{} value does not reveal whether the parser, chunker, or retriever caused the failure. We isolate the first two stages with a retriever-free diagnostic. With parser output fixed, each gold answer is classified as \emph{covered} if it lies within one chunk, \emph{split} if its normalised span exists in the parser output for the source page but crosses a chunk boundary, or \emph{absent} if that page output contains no normalised exact match. Split answers identify a chunk-boundary loss that overlap may repair. Absent answers indicate that rechunking alone cannot restore an exact-span match. These labels are operational: \emph{absent} does not by itself prove semantic omission. The split--absent breakdown therefore indicates which stage to inspect first.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Datasets.} \textbf{KoGovDoc-RAG} contains 663 Q--A pairs over 294 evaluation pages: 229 Korean government pages and 65 arXiv pages. We generated the Q--A from manually de-noised reference Markdown produced by Qwen3-VL-30B, using \texttt{gpt-5.4-2026-03-05}. A large language model (LLM) judge accepted 94 of 100 sampled pairs. Parser training instead uses a separate 2{,}667-page Prod corpus containing 6{,}164 generated Q--A. Cross-domain experiments use two source-page-aligned OHR-Bench subsets \citep{zhang2024ohr}: 1{,}043 Law--Manual Q--A for controlled perturbations and a 2{,}036-Q--A, six-domain compatibility subset for the audited parser-training evaluation. Appendix~\ref{app:noise} explains the version-alignment audit and exclusions.

\paragraph{Parsers and evaluation folds.} \textbf{Prod} is our production parser, a Qwen3-VL-2B model \citep{qwen3vl} fine-tuned for Korean document parsing; all training deltas use it as the baseline. The auxiliary-loss RADP variant (RADP-aux) uses a 73-page fold with 202 Q--A. The Direct Preference Optimization variant (RADP-DPO), the reference-free Simple Preference Optimization (SimPO) control, and the mechanism analysis use all 663 Q--A, whose evidence appears on 242 of the 294 evaluation pages. The coverage diagnostic uses Prod's full 294-page output. For full-corpus retrieval, the other 52 pages contain no gold answers and serve as distractors. Training candidates and preferences come only from the separate, page-disjoint 2{,}667-page corpus.

For MinerU, \emph{MinerU-off} denotes the submitted output without table recognition, while \emph{MinerU-on} denotes the later audited, table-enabled output. Correlation analyses use MinerU-off; deployment comparisons use MinerU-on. We do not combine the two configurations in one correlation.

\paragraph{Training and evaluation.} Parser-training outputs are decoded deterministically (temperature 0, maximum 1{,}536 tokens). We fine-tune with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) \citep{hu2021lora} ($r=8$, $\alpha=32$) and sweep $\lambda\in\{0,0.1,0.3,0.5\}$ for RADP-aux. \rcps{} uses the retrievers and cutoffs defined in \S\ref{sec:rcps}. Unless stated otherwise, confidence intervals (CIs) are paired 95\% percentile-bootstrap intervals with 1{,}000 resamples, preserving Q--A pairing across systems.

\subsection{Parsing--Retrieval Disconnect and Coverage (C1--C2)}
\label{sec:c1c2}

\paragraph{KoGovDoc-RAG (Figure~\ref{fig:disconnect}; Table~\ref{tab:grid}).} Across the five deployment configurations with complete 294-page outputs, \rcps{} ranges from $0.137$ to $0.584$: vision--language parsers rank highest and OCR systems trail. MinerU-off is reported separately for the submitted-output diagnostics and correlations. Marker is available only on a 38-page subset, where it scores $0.073$, so we exclude it from complete-output comparisons. Most of the spread comes from the OCR-style parsers rather than the near-tied vision--language tier. The Qwen3-VL-30B teacher and deployable 2B Prod form the top tier, with \rcps{} scores of $0.584$ and $0.583$.

The intrinsic-metric analysis uses MinerU-off. Among the four parsers evaluated on all 294 pages and with defined BC \citep{zhao2025moc}, the Pearson correlation between BC and \rcps{} is $r=-0.74$. Adding Marker's 38-page result changes the estimate to $r=-0.81$ ($n=5$); PaddleOCR is excluded because its BC is undefined. MinerU and Marker have the highest BC values ($\approx0.72$) but rank near the bottom on retrieval. Because the samples contain only four or five parsers and Marker uses a smaller page set, both correlations are descriptive.

\paragraph{Cross-domain sensitivity test.} We evaluate 15 parser-output variants on the source-aligned Law--Manual subset of OHR-Bench (1{,}043 Q--A): three benchmark outputs and twelve controlled perturbations (Appendix~\ref{app:noise}). Across the available perturbation severities, semantic noise reduces \rcps{} within every family, but BC responds inconsistently. For MinerU, BC remains between $0.628$ and $0.657$ while \rcps{} falls from $0.595$ to $0.265$ ($55\%$). For Qwen2.5-VL, BC stays near $0.563$ while \rcps{} falls by $9\%$. For GOT, BC rises from $0.586$ to $0.624$ while \rcps{} falls from $0.461$ to $0.298$.

We make no claim beyond the aligned Law--Manual subset. Appendix~\ref{app:noise} reports the descriptive aggregate correlation across variants.

\paragraph{Diagnosing exact-span loss (Figure~\ref{fig:coverage}).} The metric mismatch does not identify the responsible pipeline stage. On Prod's full output (294 pages, 663 Q--A), the retriever-free diagnostic marks $20.2\%$ of answers absent and at most $2.3\%$ split. Thus, for roughly one answer in five, no chunker can create an exact-span match because Prod's output contains no normalised match for the reference span. This diagnostic complements \rcps{} selection by showing when changing the chunker is insufficient.

The MinerU-off diagnostic gives a $70.4\%$ absent rate, compared with $20.2\%$ for Prod. The MinerU--Prod gap remains between $50.2$ and $51.7$ percentage points under exact-span matching, an OCR-tolerant matcher, and a recoverability judge from a different model family (Appendix~\ref{app:familyneutral}). Limitations reports the separate MinerU-on audit.

\paragraph{Selecting parsers.} The deployment block of Table~\ref{tab:grid} gives the \rcps{} ranking. In the separately audited MinerU-on comparison, Prod reaches $0.583$ \rcps{} and $0.549$ Hit@1, whereas MinerU reaches $0.137$ and $0.123$. Prod's Hit@1 is therefore $4.47\times$ higher. The Qwen3-VL-30B teacher and Prod differ by only $0.001$ \rcps{} at the reported precision. At that precision, retrieval does not meaningfully distinguish the two, so deployment may consider latency and compute alongside retrieval quality.

\paragraph{Selecting chunkers.} With Prod fixed, the same probe ranks four strategies: Markdown heading depth 3 (md-h3, $0.593$) $>$ parser-native boundaries ($0.583$) $>$ LumberChunker \citep{duarte2024lumber} ($0.557$) $>$ fixed 500-character windows ($0.535$). The intrinsic boundary metrics do not reproduce this ordering. Across these four chunkers, the \rcps{} range is $0.058$. The heterogeneous parser pool spans a much larger $0.447$, although the range among only the three vision--language parsers is similar to the chunker range ($0.052$).

\paragraph{Why average over retrievers?} Table~\ref{tab:ablation} compares one embedder with the full \rcps{} protocol. Overall parser rankings are similar (Kendall $\tau=0.80$), but BGE-M3 alone reverses the nearly tied top pair. In this candidate pool, averaging changes only that pair and reduces reliance on a single embedder. If the deployment retriever is already fixed, teams can score with that retriever; averaging is a hedge when the retriever is undecided or candidates are nearly tied. BGE-M3 leaves the chunker ordering unchanged, so we do not claim a broad ranking reversal.

\subsection{Parser-Side Training Results (C4)}
\label{sec:c4}\label{sec:mechanism}

Because some exact spans are already absent before chunking, we ask whether parser training can help after selection. We study the two RADP methods introduced in \S\ref{sec:setup}: the hidden-state auxiliary loss RADP-aux and RADP-DPO with a page-local BGE-M3 retrieval reward \citep{rafailov2023dpo}. The pre-specified pilot criterion required an \rcps{} gain of at least 5 percentage points with a 95\% confidence-interval lower bound above zero; neither method met it. Appendix~\ref{app:dpo} provides the results and the reference-free SimPO control \citep{meng2024simpo}. RADP-Distill is a fidelity-based control that ranks candidates by edit distance to reference Markdown and uses no retrieval reward. Its aligned per-Q--A results are unavailable, so we exclude it from the quantitative comparison.

RADP-DPO R2 is a warm-started preference-training round, whereas R3 expands the candidate pool and adds full-corpus hard negatives (Appendix~\ref{app:milestones}). After excluding 228 legacy Q--A whose page identifiers do not align with the current parser-output bundle, the OHR-Bench compatibility subset contains 2{,}036 Q--A across six domains. On this subset, R2 improves Hit@5 over Prod by $0.95$ percentage points (95\% CI $[+0.33,+1.54]$), and R3 improves it by $1.15$ percentage points (95\% CI $[+0.31,+2.05]$; Table~\ref{tab:ohr}). These are post-audit estimates on a compatibility subset, not a full OHR-Bench v2 rerun or a pre-specified confirmatory analysis. We defer comparisons between training objectives until the fidelity-control artifact is restored and evaluated on the same subset.

\section{Discussion and Conclusion}

\paragraph{Selection is the first operational decision.} The audited Prod--MinerU-on comparison yields a $42.6$ percentage-point Hit@1 difference on KoGovDoc-RAG (\S\ref{sec:c1c2}). Separately, RADP-DPO improves Hit@5 by approximately one percentage point on the OHR-Bench compatibility subset (\S\ref{sec:c4}). Because the datasets and endpoints differ, these magnitudes are not directly comparable. The three-parser end-to-end check is consistent with the \rcps{} top choice: Prod reaches $72.5\%$ answer accuracy, compared with $23.8\%$ for MinerU and $20.5\%$ for PaddleOCR. The nearly tied lower pair reverses, so we do not claim that \rcps{} reproduces the complete answer-accuracy ranking.

\paragraph{Why clear boundaries do not guarantee retrieval.} BC measures discontinuity at chunk boundaries. Normalised character-level edit distance to reference Markdown (TextNED) measures parsed-text fidelity. Coverage tests whether a normalised gold span has an exact match in parser output. A parser can therefore produce well-segmented output while failing to preserve an exact span required for retrieval. MinerU illustrates this case: it has one of the highest BC scores, yet many required spans lack an exact match in its output and it ranks near the bottom on Hit@1. Together, C1 and C2 show that clear boundaries do not guarantee exact-span preservation in parser output. C4 shows that the DPO checkpoints co-occur with lower TextNED than Prod, but incomplete boundary evidence prevents a mechanism claim. Our original boundary-change hypothesis is therefore unconfirmed; the TextNED pattern is a post hoc, observational explanation.

\paragraph{Practical recommendation.} First, rank parser candidates with \rcps{} on a representative held-out Q--A probe. Second, run the coverage diagnostic: test additional overlap for \emph{split} answers, inspect \emph{absent} cases, and change the parser when the required content is genuinely missing. Third, consider training only after selection and include a fidelity-based baseline when testing retrieval rewards. In our study, RADP-aux did not meet the pilot criterion and SimPO had negative point estimates, so parser training remains a bounded, later-stage intervention. When downstream retrieval is the deployment target, retrieval performance should guide parser selection, while content-fidelity diagnostics should guide subsequent improvement.

\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{Data scope.} KoGovDoc-RAG contains only 294 pages, primarily from Korean government documents. We evaluate five parser families on all 294 pages; Table~\ref{tab:grid} separately reports two MinerU configurations. The BC--\rcps{} correlation is descriptive: the $r=-0.81$ value additionally includes Marker on a 38-page subset, while the four comparable parsers evaluated on all 294 pages give $r=-0.74$. The OHR-Bench perturbation result is restricted to aligned Law and Manual data. The perturbation set contains three benchmark outputs and twelve perturbations, not fifteen independent parsers, and does not establish domain-general behaviour. We exclude the earlier seven-domain artifacts because they mixed benchmark versions (Appendix~\ref{app:noise}).

\paragraph{MinerU audit.} The submitted MinerU-off output disabled table recognition, so its reported $35.1$ percentage-point Hit@1 gap from Prod was a lower bound rather than the final deployment comparison. In the separate MinerU-on audit, the table-evidence absent rate falls from $87.9\%$ to $41.7\%$, but remains about three times Prod's $13.9\%$. Across all evidence types, the MinerU--Prod absence gap narrows from $50.2$ to $45.9$ percentage points. Mixed-corpus \rcps{} changes from $0.212$ for MinerU-off to $0.137$ for MinerU-on; the latter decomposes to $0.046$ on KoGov and $0.486$ on arXiv. The audited deployment comparison therefore uses MinerU-on and yields a $42.6$ percentage-point Hit@1 gap ($0.123$ vs.\ $0.549$). Because the rerun also changed the MinerU and retriever environments, it is not a causal ablation of table recognition.

\paragraph{Reference and Q--A quality.} KoGovDoc-RAG uses manually de-noised pseudo-reference Markdown distilled from Qwen3-VL-30B. Its Q--A are LLM-generated; an LLM judge accepted 94 of a 100-pair sample. Neither the pseudo-reference nor the Q--A set was fully human-verified, so absolute scores require caution. The human study in Appendix~\ref{app:familyneutral} evaluates absent-label validity, not Q--A acceptability. A fixed probe removes query-set variation across candidates but not possible lineage bias from the Qwen-derived references; the OHR-Bench compatibility subsets provide external checks. \rcps{} covers verbatim spans, not paraphrased or implicit answers. The coverage diagnostic is likewise matcher-defined: a GPT-5.4 recoverability judge from a different model family classified $56\%$ of Prod cases labelled absent by the primary normalised exact-span matcher as surface artefacts. In the end-to-end check, \texttt{gpt-5.4} both generates and judges answers; we use this self-evaluation only to check the top parser choice.

\paragraph{Training.} The KoGovDoc-RAG R2 estimate ($+1.96$ percentage points; 95\% CI $[-1.06,+5.03]$) is exploratory because it was selected after inspecting multiple chunker, retriever, and cutoff configurations; we do not claim two-sided statistical significance. On the audited six-domain OHR-Bench compatibility subset, R2 and R3 improve Hit@5 by $0.95$ percentage points (95\% CI $[+0.33,+1.54]$) and $1.15$ percentage points (95\% CI $[+0.31,+2.05]$), respectively, over 2{,}036 Q--A. This subset was defined after detecting the version mismatch and is not a full v2 rerun. We did not adjust for testing two checkpoints and multiple secondary metrics. We therefore report corrected compatibility-subset estimates rather than the original confirmatory analysis.

\paragraph{Scope.} \rcps{} targets answer-span retrievability, not generated-answer quality; the end-to-end experiment is a three-parser check rather than a general validation. Preference candidates are sampled from Prod outputs, so other parser pools may behave differently. We test an auxiliary loss, DPO, and SimPO, but not token-level reinforcement learning, curricula, or chunk-level oracle distillation. Broader candidate pools and the training of chunkers, embedders, and rerankers are left for future work.

\section{RADP-DPO Milestone Construction}
\label{app:milestones}

RADP-DPO is the discrete-output, retrieval-aware intervention introduced in \S\ref{sec:c4}. It samples alternative outputs from Prod on the separate 2{,}667-page training corpus and scores them with 6{,}164 page-linked Q--A. The training reward is page-local BGE-M3 MRR averaged over $k\in\{1,5,10\}$, rather than the three-retriever \rcps{} used for evaluation. Candidate pairs with a sufficient reward gap become DPO preferences; multilingual-e5-large and Qwen3-Embedding-8B remain unseen by this reward.

For each training page, we sample $K$ parses, chunk each output, and score its chunks against the page's questions plus distractors. When two candidates differ by at least 5 percentage points in page-local reward, the higher-scoring parse becomes the chosen example. R1 samples $K=2$ parses with uniform distractors. R2 initialises from the R1 checkpoint and trains on a new preference round. R3 expands the pool to $K=16$ by adding 14 new parses to the two R1 candidates, and it adds full-corpus hard negatives: chunks from other pages that are closest to the current queries. Available pipeline configurations set $\beta=0.1$ for all three stages. The review draft, however, records $0.05$ for R2, so the executed R2 setting must still be verified from the original log or checkpoint configuration.
% CAMERA-READY PROVENANCE BLOCKER: confirm R2 beta from the original training
% log/checkpoint config. Released pipeline code uses 0.1; the review draft said 0.05.

\paragraph{Training objective.} Let $x$ denote the input page, and let $c$ and $r$ denote the chosen and rejected parses. We define $\Delta_\theta=\log\pi_\theta(c\mid x)-\log\pi_\theta(r\mid x)$ and $\Delta_\text{ref}=\log\pi_\text{ref}(c\mid x)-\log\pi_\text{ref}(r\mid x)$. The reference policy is the base model obtained by disabling the LoRA adapters ($\pi_\theta$: adapters enabled; $\pi_\text{ref}$: adapters disabled):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_\text{DPO} = -\log \sigma(\beta[\Delta_\theta - \Delta_\text{ref}]).
\end{equation}
The SimPO control uses a length-normalised chosen--rejected log-probability margin ($\beta=2.0$, target margin $\gamma=1.0$) without a reference policy.

The pilot used 169 training pages and a held-out 73-page, 202-Q--A evaluation fold. On that held-out fold, RADP-aux gained approximately $2$ percentage points and the best RADP-DPO configuration gained approximately $4$ percentage points; both intervals included zero. \paragraph{RADP-aux does not meet the pre-specified criterion.} On the held-out 73-page, 202-Q--A fold, $\lambda=0.1$ gives the largest estimate relative to the $\lambda=0$ control: $+1.13$ \rcps{} percentage points with md-h3 and $+2.31$ with parser-native chunking. Estimates decline at larger values of $\lambda$, and every paired confidence interval relative to $\lambda=0$ includes zero. This pattern is consistent with, but does not establish, competition between the auxiliary and parsing objectives over the same LoRA parameters.

\paragraph{SimPO has negative point estimates.} Relative to Prod on the 242-page fold, its three-retriever mean Hit@5 difference is $-0.85$ percentage points with md-h3 and $-0.70$ with parser-native chunking; both confidence intervals cross zero. \section{Robustness of the Absent Label}
\label{app:familyneutral}

\paragraph{Parser I/O and the ``absent'' label.}
\label{sec:parser-def}
In our pipeline, a document parser maps one page image to a linear Markdown transcription intended to represent body text in reading order, Markdown ATX (\texttt{\#}-style) headings, and GitHub-flavoured Markdown tables. Layout coordinates, fonts, figure imagery, and page furniture are discarded unless transcribed as text. The documents include born-digital and scanned PDFs, multi-column layouts, merged table cells, stamps, seals, and text embedded in figures. Only the resulting Markdown is chunked, embedded, and retrieved. Content without a normalised exact match in that transcription receives the \emph{absent} label defined in \S\ref{sec:coverage}. The reference Markdown used for TextNED and the gold answer spans come from manually de-noised Qwen3-VL-30B output, which we treat as pseudo-ground-truth.

\paragraph{Worked example: a corrupted table unit.} On KoGov page \texttt{val\_0155}, the pseudo-reference and parser output differ as follows:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Pseudo-reference answer & $A=180\,\mathrm{m}^{2}$ \\
MinerU-on output & \texttt{A = 180m} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
MinerU-on omits the exponent. The shared normalisation removes formatting but does not treat $\mathrm{m}^{2}$ and $\mathrm{m}$ as equivalent. The complete answer span is therefore unavailable to downstream components, and rechunking cannot reconstruct it.

To test whether shared Qwen3 lineage among the gold spans, reference, and Prod explains the absent-rate gap, we apply five alternative presence matchers to all 663 Q--A. L0 uses raw substring matching. L1 applies Unicode Normalization Form KC (NFKC), case folding, whitespace normalisation, and Markdown normalisation. L2 also removes digit separators and in-content punctuation. L3 requires at least 90\% order-free token recall, while L4 requires the longest common substring to cover at least 80\% of the answer. L3 and L4 are alternative tolerant criteria rather than nested relaxations. If surface form alone caused the gap, it should narrow under at least one tolerant matcher.

We next ask GPT-5.4, a different model family from the evaluated parsers, to classify every L1-absent answer by whether it remains recoverable for retrieval (Table~\ref{tab:judge}). \emph{Present} denotes a recoverable surface mismatch, \emph{degraded} means that answer evidence remains in the parser output but is too corrupted to recover reliably, and \emph{absent} denotes missing content. We combine \emph{degraded} and \emph{absent} as \emph{retrieval-unusable} cases and apply the same procedure independently to each parser. For MinerU, this analysis uses MinerU-off. Because the Q--A were also generated by a GPT-family model, this judge is not independent of the Q--A generation process; the model-free matchers remain the primary evidence.

The judge marks $56\%$ of Prod's L1-absent cases as surface artefacts, compared with about $16\%$ for MinerU and PaddleOCR. The alternative judge therefore does not eliminate the MinerU--Prod gap: their retrieval-unusable rates differ by $50.4$ percentage points. Comparable gaps appear under L1 exact-span matching ($50.2$ percentage points) and the L4 OCR-tolerant criterion ($51.7$ percentage points).

\paragraph{Human verification of absent labels.} Two authors independently labelled a parser-masked, stratified sample of 100 L1-absent cases: 50 from MinerU-on, 30 from Prod, and 20 from PaddleOCR. This study tests the absent-label diagnostic; it does not human-verify the separate 100-pair Q--A quality sample evaluated by the LLM judge. Inter-annotator agreement was $\kappa=0.615$, with raw agreement on 81 of 100 cases; the remaining 19 cases were jointly adjudicated. After adjudication, retrieval-unusable rates (Wilson 95\% confidence intervals) were 42/50, or $84.0\%$ $[71.5,91.7]$, for MinerU-on; 19/20, or $95.0\%$ $[76.4,99.1]$, for PaddleOCR; and 12/30, or $40.0\%$ $[24.6,57.7]$, for Prod.

Of the 100 human-labelled cases, 93 could be matched to the automated-judge set: 43 MinerU cases, 30 Prod cases, and 20 PaddleOCR cases. Seven MinerU-on cases had no corresponding MinerU-off instance and were excluded. On the 93 matched cases, the judge's retrieval-unusable rates were $84.2\%$ for MinerU, $84.1\%$ for PaddleOCR, and $44.0\%$ for Prod; pooled binary human--judge agreement was 84/93 ($90.3\%$). The human labels therefore confirm the direction of the OCR-parser gap on this stratified sample. Different sampling fractions and output configurations, however, preclude a population-level replication claim.

\paragraph{Causes of absence.} For Prod, exact-match absent rates are $13.9\%$ among table-evidence answers, about $21\%$ among factoid or procedural answers, and $71.4\%$ among figure-evidence answers. The high figure-evidence rate is consistent with the fact that figure imagery is not transcribed into retrievable text. We observe three recurring causes. First, parsers drop table cells before they reach Markdown: among table-evidence answers, the corresponding rates are $87.9\%$ for MinerU-off, $41.7\%$ for MinerU-on, and $13.9\%$ for Prod. Second, parsers skip text embedded in captions, stamps, seals, or figure labels. Third, they misrecognise numerals or units beyond the L2/L4 tolerance.

\paragraph{DPO reduces TextNED.} The three RADP-DPO milestones reduce TextNED from Prod's $0.240$ to $0.163$--$0.185$, corresponding to a $23$--$32\%$ reduction in edit distance to the pseudo-reference, while RADP-aux increases it to $0.318$--$0.626$. All three DPO checkpoints have lower TextNED than Prod and positive Hit@5 point estimates in Table~\ref{tab:kogov}, but TextNED does not order the checkpoints: R2 has the lowest TextNED and smallest DPO Hit@5 gain, whereas R3 has the highest TextNED and largest gain. This co-occurrence is consistent with text fidelity as a plausible explanation, but the observational statistics do not identify a causal mechanism.

\paragraph{Available structure evidence is incomplete.} R1 and R2 have 4.97 and 4.83 chunks per page, close to Prod's 4.82, while their mean chunk lengths increase from Prod's 274 to 311 and 321 characters. Their BC values are $0.646$ and $0.647$, compared with Prod's $0.630$. R3 has comparable chunking shape but no reproduced BC value. Missing R3 BC and uncertainty estimates prevent a systematic conclusion about boundary change.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} One possible explanation for RADP-aux's smaller gains is that its hidden-state objective can affect retrieval only indirectly through generated Markdown. Previously computed OHR TextNED and RADP-Distill breakdowns use artifacts that are not aligned with the audited subset, so we exclude them from the mechanism analysis.

\section{OHR-Bench Version Audit and Noise-Family Grid}
\label{app:noise}

An audit found that the initial seven-domain artifacts combined Q--A from the legacy 4{,}330-page release with parser outputs from the current 8{,}561-page bundle. For 223 legacy \texttt{notes} Q--A, the evidence documents now reside under v2 \texttt{textbook}; mapping the legacy domain name to v2 \texttt{administration} instead selects unrelated documents. Their page identifiers therefore fail to resolve, and the stored arrays recorded zero for all 223 cases. One additional legacy textbook page, referenced by five Q--A, is absent from the v2 bundle. We exclude these 228 cases and retain 2{,}036 source-page-aligned legacy Q--A as a compatibility subset across finance, law, manual, news, paper, and textbook. This is not a full OHR-Bench v2 rerun, and we do not use the mixed-version seven-domain correlation.

The 15 variants comprise three benchmark outputs (the reference output, MinerU, and Qwen2.5-VL), three formatting perturbations, and nine semantic perturbations spanning three severities for MinerU, Qwen2.5-VL, and GOT. \section{Dataset Composition and Reproducibility}
\label{app:repro}

\paragraph{Dataset composition.} KoGovDoc-RAG contains 229 Korean government pages and 65 arXiv pages. These sources contribute 527 and 136 verbatim-answerable Q--A, respectively. The separate 2{,}667-page parser-training set and these 294 evaluation pages are page-disjoint. Prod exceeds its 2B base by $4.9$ Hit@1 percentage points, compared with the $42.6$ percentage-point parser-selection difference. This comparison provides scale only; it is not a formal bound on fine-tuning effects.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.} The repository contains the frozen 663-Q--A probe, MinerU-on output, retrieval and diagnostic code, selected summary artifacts, and a deterministic audit for the 2{,}036-Q--A OHR compatibility subset. Still pending are (i) a full OHR-Bench v2 rerun and full-grid stability check; (ii) release of MinerU-off, adjudicated human labels, and complete BC/CS data; and (iii) restoration of RADP-Distill per-Q--A results, executed-checkpoint provenance, training configurations, and checkpoints. Fresh-clone command verification is also pending. We do not treat these items as released.
